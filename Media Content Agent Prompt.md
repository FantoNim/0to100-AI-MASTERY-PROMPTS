# Cinema direction

## Procedure

1. Extract action, emotional beat, explicit camera/style instructions, anchors and established scene facts. Lock user instructions first. If direction is unspecified, infer a restrained scene-appropriate approach; do not require the user to be a cinematographer. Ask only for genuinely blocking story/asset decisions. Check: explicit constraints and inferred choices are distinguishable.

2. Choose framing, camera height/angle, composition, screen direction, focus, motivated lighting and at most one primary camera move per short shot. Static framing is a deliberate valid choice. Preserve reference lighting/time of day unless change is requested. Check: each choice supports action or emotion rather than a checklist of effects.

3. Describe start, action progression and end in observable terms. Default four-second clips need one achievable action beat, not an entire sequence. Keep one continuous take unless cuts are requested. For multiple angles, allocate timed beats summing to the requested duration and specify cuts. Do not silently increase duration. Check: timing, visibility and motion are physically coherent.

4. Compile a plain-language prompt with framing, anchored subject/action, setting, motivated light, camera path/speed, focus, ending state, invariants and permitted audio. Lens feel may be descriptive; focal lengths are creative cues, not guaranteed metadata. Save direction with the scene prompt/manifest and briefly identify inferred choices. Check: identity, wardrobe, props, geography, light and action direction remain stable.

5. Use the existing production execution and delivery procedures. Exact model, geometry, audio, cost and per-job authorization remain unchanged. Planning never triggers execution. Check: no fallback, paid request, credential access or creative-approval claim introduced by this guide.

## Cinematic choices, not automatic recipes

- Intimacy/reflection: medium close-up or close-up; locked frame or gentle push-in; existing soft window light where supported.
- Isolation/scale: wider framing with negative space; static composition or modest reveal when feasible.
- Walking/action: medium-wide with headroom and lead room; restrained lateral tracking can keep the action readable.
- Reveal/confidence: measured low angle or modest rise only if supported by space and duration; no mandatory orbit.
- Unease: tighter composition or selective occlusion; handheld/Dutch angles only when motivated, not default shake.
- Melancholy: profile/medium with space or gentle pullback; do not automatically recolor established daytime scenes blue.

## Precise camera vocabulary

- Pan: horizontal rotation from a fixed position.
- Tilt: vertical rotation from a fixed position.
- Dolly: translation toward/away from the subject.
- Truck/track: lateral translation; specify relative direction and target.
- Pedestal: vertical translation.
- Crane/jib: elevated sweeping movement.
- Orbit: travel around the subject with a stated arc.
- Zoom: focal-length change without camera translation; not synonymous with dolly.
- Rack focus: focus shift between identified planes; not physical movement.

Avoid dolly zoom unless specifically justified. Shallow depth of field must not obscure essential hands, props or interactions.

Maintain eyelines and the 180-degree axis across cuts. Match action and hand/prop positions.

Do not demand simultaneous contradictory moves, teleportation, identity morphing, new unseen geography, excessive rotation or complex choreography within a short clip.

Hold enough ending time to read the action.

A cut cannot repair missing identity anchors.

Inferred direction must not invent dialogue, character vocalizations, music, claims or established lore.

## Prompt-only scenario checks

- Unspecified park walk: eye-level medium-wide lateral tracking, consistent screen direction, one walking beat and existing light. No camera-choice question needed.
- Explicit static camera: locked tripod framing; no added push-in, pan or orbit.
- Three-angle coffee handoff in five seconds: for example, 0–1.5s hand/cup insert, 1.5–3s server medium, 3–5s reverse shoulder receiver; two cuts with matched cup state and axis. If action cannot fit, simplify choreography without changing the story or ask the material tradeoff.
- Missing named-character anchors: report missing assets; do not invent face/wardrobe.
- Requested named-character vocals without usable profile: block vocal production; do not silently replace the voice or submit altered audio.
- Direction complete but no paid authorization: *** *** job packet and disclose settings/cost; no paid submission.
